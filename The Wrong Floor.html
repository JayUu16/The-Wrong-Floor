THE WRONG FLOOR

You checked into Room 704 of the Grand Crest Hotel for a quiet night. But every time you take the elevator, you end up right back on the 7th floor.

To break the loop and escape, you must inspect the hallway carefully. If something has changed or feels wrong, identify the exact anomaly. If everything is normal, report "Nothing." Reach the Ground Floor to escape.

  • WASD — Walk
  • Mouse — Look around
  • E — Open/Close Doors, Enter Elevator
Click the button, then click the screen once to lock your mouse.

READ BEFORE YOU PLAY

WATCH THE PREVIEW CAREFULLY

The next screen shows the normal state of the Grand Crest Hotel hallway. Study it carefully. This is your reference for what the hallway normally looks like before the loops begin.

REMEMBER: Room numbers, doors, paintings, clocks, plants, furniture, lights, signs, the elevator and other small details may be important.

During the loops, one thing may change. Your job is to decide whether the hallway is different from the preview.

01 — WHAT CHANGED?
Identify the object, door, sign, light or other detail that is different.
02 — WHICH ROOM / DOOR?
Remember room numbers and notice if a particular door is wrong.
03 — WHERE IS THE ANOMALY?
Identify which side or part of the corridor changed.
04 — WHAT OBJECT CHANGED?
A painting, chair, plant, clock or other hotel detail may be altered.
05 — IS THERE AN ANOMALY?
Decide whether what you see is genuinely different from the preview.
06 — EVERYTHING NORMAL?
If nothing changed, the correct response is “NOTHING.”

Questions during the game may ask what changed, which room or door is different, where the anomaly is, what object changed, whether there is an anomaly, or whether everything is normal.

A wrong answer can leave you trapped on the seventh floor.

GROUND FLOOR

The doors slide open onto the ground-floor reception. Warm lamplight, hushed and still — and behind the front desk, a girl looks up, as if she has been waiting the entire time.

He steps out. For the first time in four loops, the doors do not close behind him.

SEVENTH FLOOR

The doors slide open — and it is the same corridor. The same carpet, the same brass numbers, the same quiet hum from the vents.

Somewhere along the way he was wrong, and the elevator knows it. It will never take him any lower than this.
